Signature Franchise 02

The Silk Question.

One question. Multiple women. Conversation and shareability — the format built for the answer you screenshot and send to a friend.

One question, asked differently every time

Every round of The Silk Question starts the same way: one question, put to several women who don't know what the others said. No panel, no consensus — just the same prompt landing in different lives.

What comes back is never one answer. It's a spread of them, side by side, so you can see how differently the same question lands depending on who's holding it.

The best answers travel on their own — that's the format doing its job.

"One question. Multiple women. Conversation and shareability."

How it works

  1. One question drops. Short, specific, never a yes-or-no — the kind of question that has a different answer depending on who you ask.

  2. Multiple women answer. Each one writes her answer without seeing anyone else's, in her own words, as long or short as it needs to be.

  3. The answers run together. Published side by side, so the contrast is the point — the same question, several honest answers.

  4. Readers pick a favorite. Share the one that sounds like you, or the one that surprised you — that's what carries the format past the page.

What a round looks like

An illustrative round, to show the shape of it — not a published edition.

"What's something you spent money on that people would judge, but you'd do again?"

  • Woman, 26 — designer

    A flight home for a Tuesday dinner. Nobody needs to understand it, I just needed to be there.

  • Woman, 34 — founder

    A therapist I couldn't technically afford for the first six months. Worth every uncomfortable invoice.

  • Woman, 22 — student

    A really good coat. It's the only thing I own that makes me feel like I have my life together.
